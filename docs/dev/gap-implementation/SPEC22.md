## Why

The roll thermal model has one row of identical nozzles with a uniform ("top-hat") footprint, a
footprint width from geometry only, and cooling linear in the flow level. The research found:
makers call the fan profile parabolic or tapered [SN2; SN5]; catalogue pattern widths are 6-14 %
wider than the geometric one [SN5; computed]; vendor overlap practice gives a footprint of 1.1 to
1.5 nozzle pitches where the demo headers have 3.2; and cooling rises as flow to the power 0.5 to
1.0 [computed from water data and one kerosene CFD]. A parabolic footprint at the same flow makes
the deficit of a blocked nozzle about 1.5 times deeper and 0.7 times as wide [computed].

The owner approved: these as OPT-IN options, the existing demos left exactly as they are, and ONE
new demo with a realistic header. Several rows per roll, valve types and hot-edge rows are Phase
3 and are out of scope here.

## Part 1 — header options (`roll_thermal.SprayHeader` and everything that reads it)

New optional keys of `width_model.spray_header`, all with defaults that reproduce today's
numbers bit for bit:

| key | meaning | default | range, validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `footprint_profile` | shape of one nozzle's cooling along the barrel | `"tophat"` | `"tophat"` or `"parabolic"`; message names the two |
| `footprint_width_m` | pattern width along the barrel as built (catalogue width at the standoff), replacing the geometric width | `null` = geometric | > 0, <= 5; message says "the pattern width along the barrel" |
| `htc_flow_exponent` | cooling of a nozzle at flow level s is `s ** n` | `1.0` | 0.5 to 1.0 |

- `parabolic`: density `1.5 * (1 - (2x/W)**2)` on `[-W/2, W/2]`, zero outside, so that it
  integrates to the same total as the top-hat of width `W` and a healthy header still averages to
  1 away from the barrel ends. Cell averages are taken with the exact integral of the parabola
  over each cell (closed form, no quadrature). Comment and result basis: "parabolic is the makers'
  word [SN2 FT; SN5 VP]; the function is a stand-in, no measured profile is published".
- `effective_footprint_width_m` returns `footprint_width_m` when given, else the geometric value;
  every reader of the header (the bulk model's weight profile, the surface layer, `ControlledRun`,
  the influence matrix, `band_signatures.spray_coverage`, `band_width.spray_layout`, the nozzle
  map, the guided app's drawing) must take the width from that one property. Search for any place
  that recomputes `2*standoff*tan(angle/2)*cos(offset)` on its own and route it through the
  property; list what you found.
- `htc_flow_exponent`: applied where a nozzle's level becomes its weight; with `n = 1` the code
  path and the numbers are unchanged.
- The offset-angle limit in `SprayHeader.validate` goes from 60 to 70 degrees [SN5 VP sells 70].
- Result: `width.thermal_steady` (and `spray_layout`) gain `footprint_profile`,
  `footprint_width_source` (`geometric` / `supplied`), `footprint_width_over_pitch` and
  `htc_flow_exponent`. Flags, each with its `band_plain.FLAGS` sentence and register row:
  `FOOTPRINT_PROFILE_STAND_IN` (set when the profile is not the default),
  `SPRAY_FLOW_EXPONENT_APPLIED` (set when n is not 1),
  `FOOTPRINT_WIDER_THAN_VENDOR_PRACTICE` (set when width over pitch exceeds 1.5: "fans overlap
  more than makers recommend (1.1 to 1.5 nozzle pitches [SN2; SN7; SN13]); a blocked nozzle is
  smoothed more than on such a header" — this flag WILL appear in the existing v2 demos, whose
  headers have 3.2; that is intended and is the honest label for them).
- When any nozzle level lies strictly between 0 and 1 and n is 1, a note in the section:
  "cooling is taken as proportional to flow; published exponents are 0.5 to 1.0, with which the
  stripe of this partly blocked nozzle would be 7 to 45 % smaller [computed]".

## Part 3 — corrections the research found (text and one flag)

1. The footprint formula's tag in `roll_thermal.py` and guide 7.x: `[V: Lloyd 2010; Tucker 2006]`
   becomes `[D] geometric; neither paper prints it [SN1 FT; SN3 FT]; catalogue widths are 6-14 %
   larger [SN5 VP; computed]`.
2. `roll_thermal._LIMITATIONS` and the guide: "the nozzle-pattern ripple is an artefact" becomes
   "the nozzle-pattern ripple is of unknown size: with a parabolic profile a healthy header still
   ripples by about -12 % / +7 % at 1.43 pitches and about +/-5 % at 3.2 [computed]; the top-hat
   value is not representative".
3. Overlap: the guide and the `spray_coverage` note say "definition varies between sources;
   10-30 % or 10-50 % of the pattern width [PT-T21; PT-T22; SN7; SN13]". The rule and its
   thresholds do NOT change.
4. The spray coefficient basis (X3) is now settled as far as the sources allow: the published
   kerosene value is an average over the roll-wall patch of one cooling zone [SN14 FT], so it is
   not comparable with the model's circumferential mean. In `band_thermal`:
   `basis_comparable` becomes `"NOT_COMPARABLE"` with the reason and the source; the flag
   `SPRAY_COEFFICIENT_BASIS_NOT_ESTABLISHED` is renamed
   `SPRAY_COEFFICIENT_BASIS_NOT_COMPARABLE` (update `band_plain.FLAGS`, the register's flag
   mapping and row, the guide, every test). The verdict wording must still not read as agreement.
   With a wetted arc the existing conversion stays; add the computed range "for arcs of 20 to 90
   degrees the circumferential mean of the published value is 0.14 to 1.07 kW/m2K [computed]".
5. The product catalogue / process-temperature facts built from PT-T18 and PT-T19
   (`band_products.py` and the generated `docs/thin-gauge-products.md`): PT-T18 is two rows of 34
   nozzles at 52 mm per work roll, entry side only, kerosene, and its rise from 3.0 to 4.0
   kW/m2K is in time, not across space [SN14 FT]; PT-T19's own conclusions say "about 3-5 times",
   its text 3-6 [SN16 FT]: cite both. Change at the generator, regenerate, never edit the
   generated file. The delivered research reports are not edited: add one bullet per correction to
   "Statements later corrected" in `docs/research/band-screening-2026-11/README.md`.
6. Gap study section 11, row A5: add that cooling is "between flow to the power 0.5 and 1.0, for
   water and one kerosene CFD", that the footprint options exist, and that rows, valve types and
   hot-edge sprays are planned with the roll stack phase.

## Proof (end of package)

1. Defaults reproduce today's numbers: with none of the three keys set, `weight_profile` returns
   the same floats as before for the two v2 demos' headers (assert equality, not closeness, in a
   unit test against values computed by the old formula kept in the test).

   parabolic header averages to 1 away from the ends; one blocked nozzle at 1.43 pitches gives a

   1.43 pitches for the top-hat, and 0.47 against 0.31 at 3.21 pitches (the research's computed
