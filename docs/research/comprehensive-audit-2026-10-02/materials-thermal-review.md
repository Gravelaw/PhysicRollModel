# Material, thermal, lubrication and damage evidence review

Audit date: 2026-10-02. Scope: original bibliography R049–R079 and R089–R093, plus the separate Olaogun AA8015 journal article. This is a research and validation plan. It does not authorize implementing all proposed models, deploying a plant system or operating equipment.

## Decisions supported by the evidence

The next useful material model is a bounded, experimentally identified flow-stress model with explicit alloy, temper, loading mode, strain, strain-rate and temperature coverage. The existing synthetic hardening law is a useful verification fixture, but it cannot support physical route selection. Start with one evidence-backed alloy and process window; do not force the illustrative 6 mm to 0.05 mm route into the calibration programme. Preserve the distinction between a material state, an equipment property and a pass control setting.

Temperature prediction and temperature feedback are separate milestones. First establish a conservative, passive thermal calculation with traceable contact and cooling parameters; then validate predicted temperatures; only then couple temperature back into flow stress and force. Current material-history and energy handoff interfaces provide a useful structure, but their existence does not establish these physical capabilities.

Recovery, recrystallisation, precipitation, texture and damage need different state variables and experiments. They should not be represented by silently resetting accumulated strain after a temperature threshold. The accessible AA6016 reduced FE chapter is particularly useful because it exposes this limitation: its anneal resets history, while inlet temperatures and some comparison outputs are supplied externally. Its force comparison supports a reduced-model research direction, not validation of an autonomous thermal and annealing chain. [R049, chapter §§2–3](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-58006-2_2)

## Evidence synthesis

### Flow stress, internal state and anisotropy

R063 and R064 were upgraded to substantive partial-text reviews using publicly available CC BY article text. R063's hardness-based inference needs a strain-dependent hardness-to-flow-stress conversion; similar hardness does not imply the same inferred dislocation state. R064 provides material and processing-state-specific compression fits and FE comparisons across a temperature range. Its annealed, ECAP and heat-treated specimen states are experimental inputs, not a validated kinetic evolution law. Both are useful calibration-method evidence; neither is a transferable rolling material card. [R063, §§3–6](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393758841_Characterizing_the_Substructural_Changes_in_Metals_by_Microindentation_and_Various_Numerical_Approaches), [R064, §§2–3 and comparisons](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309219274_A_Proposal_of_a_Constitutive_Description_for_Aluminium_Alloys_in_Both_Cold_and_Hot_Working)

The 6xxx evidence shows why temper cannot be reduced to a string attached to a universal power law. R065 connects texture and microstructure to hardening; R066's loading reversals distinguish isotropic strength changes from backstress. R067 adds precipitation interactions for a particular 7xxx condition. These currently abstract-level sources justify targeted investigation and required observables, not direct parameter adoption. [R065](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/0198cc85b77e-ab150c35-8682-4c99-b571-9457e7fca0c4/), [R066](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/0198cc80eff1-cab0d0b0-0599-42d1-81e5-4cd1d24df0fc/), [R067](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/interactions-between-plastic-deformation-and-precipitation-in-alu/)

Recovery and static recrystallisation deserve an explicit time-temperature module. R068 reports an AA5754 internal-state treatment with separate calibration and heating-history comparison; full parameter reconstruction still needs the article. R069 treats continuous dynamic recrystallisation in commercial-purity aluminium under severe deformation, which is a different mechanism and process domain. R070 and R078 further argue against universal grain-size or Mg multipliers across accumulated strain. [R068](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1179/026708303225005980), [R069](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/modeling-of-continuous-dynamic-recrystallization-in-commercial-pu/), [R070](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/the-effect-of-grain-size-and-strain-on-the-tensile-flow-stress-of/), [R078](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11661-006-0143-6)

A plane-stress sheet yield function such as R072 cannot simply replace a three-dimensional yield surface inside a compressed roll bite. R090 provides a relevant 3D alternative using crystal-plasticity-derived behaviour, but its published abstract's applications do not validate this project's rolling solver. R071 and R089 remain access priorities for dislocation-induced anisotropy and reliable stress integration. [R072](https://dro.deakin.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Plane_stress_yield_function_for_aluminum_alloy_sheets_-_part_1_theory/20864413), [R090](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-3d-phenomenological-yield-function-with-both-in-and-out-of-plan/)

### Heat, lubrication and cooling

R062 is a useful, openly inspectable AA5052 force/forward-slip benchmark candidate, but it is not yet a clean equation specification. Equation 6 calls R a diameter while Table 1 labels a roll radius; Equation 3 uses weighted strain history; the friction branches and work expressions need dimensional and threshold clarification. Its force/slip comparison does not establish measured thermal agreement. Preserve both the published expression and any proposed correction in a discrepancy record; never silently select whichever interpretation produces agreement. [R062, equations 1–10 and Tables 1–2](https://www.iieta.org/journals/ijht/paper/10.18280/ijht.330113)

Thermal bookkeeping needs an explicit system boundary. Conductive contact exchanges equal and opposite heat; frictional dissipation supplies an additional nonnegative source whose partition must be defined. A fitted effective heat-transfer coefficient may already absorb frictional heating, making an added source double counting. The official Abaqus documentation distinguishes these terms and software defaults; those defaults are not aluminium measurements. Fully coupled analysis is appropriate when thermal softening materially alters mechanics, but weakly coupled iteration may suffice in a validated weak-feedback domain. [R060, thermal contact](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEITNRefMap/simaitn-c-thermalinteraction.htm), [R052, coupled analysis](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-couptempdisp.htm)

R053 offers actual cooling-rig observations and an inverse heat-transfer method, but on a steel block with particular nozzles and fluids. Its coefficients cannot be transplanted to aluminium strip or a rotating roll. R054 identifies coolant formulation, temperature and geometry as relevant variables; its abstract does not supply a calibrated response surface. R061 describes zoned roll-cooling hardware and control arrangements, which inform an equipment schema rather than performance limits. [R053, pp.81–89](https://www.engineeringmechanics.cz/pdf/18_2_081.pdf), [R054](https://www.confer.cz/metal/2024/4875-cold-rolling-cooling-enhancement-and-optimization), [R061, brochure](https://www.lechler.com/fileadmin/media/kataloge/pdfs/metallurgy/EN/lechler_brochure_selectospray_en.pdf)

Mixed lubrication models R056–R057 make roughness, entrainment and real contact fraction part of friction prediction. R093's measured-film abstract also cautions that uneven supply matters. Reynolds-film and cavitation documentation can support numerical verification, but a generic journal-bearing example does not establish rolling applicability. A single fitted Coulomb coefficient remains a useful bounded first model if its experimental domain is explicit; it must not masquerade as a prediction across coolant, speed, roughness and foil regimes. [R056](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1243/PIME_PROC_1990_204_064_02), [R057](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11249-006-9021-z), [R093](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1243/PIME_PROC_1990_204_065_02), [R058](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.rotor/rotor_ug_hydrodynamicbearing.8.03.html), [R059](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.cfd.journal_bearing_cavitation/journal_bearing_cavitation.html)

### Damage, surface state and temperature measurements

R075 demonstrates load-path, triaxiality and Lode dependence in an AA2024-T351 multiaxial programme. These data are a useful constitutive/damage submodel challenge, not a validated rolling failure envelope. R073 and R074 are the closer edge-cracking and cold-rolled sheet priorities but currently lack accessible full text; R076's nonlocal formulation additionally requires mesh and length-scale validation. Damage should remain a research output until both the plasticity field and failure law have independent evidence. [R075, selected equations and comparisons](https://old.mohr.ethz.ch/papers/47.pdf)

Native surface oxide, engineered coatings, roughness and lubrication should be tracked separately. R092's anodised-strip observations cannot supply a universal safe reduction threshold for ordinary oxide-covered aluminium. R079 remains an access gap for surface deformation. [R092](https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/en/publications/surface-oxide-fracture-in-cold-aluminium-rolling/)

R091's polished-aluminium emissivity and multispectral-thermometry experiments show why an infrared temperature is a calibrated measurement model, not ground truth independent of surface condition. Its 600–800 K experiments do not establish accuracy on a colder, moving, lubricated strip. Temperature validation needs emissivity, reflected radiation, viewing geometry, time response and surface history recorded with the sensor stream. [R091, equations 1–5 and Tables 2–3](https://engineering.purdue.edu/mudawar/files/articles-all/2005/2005_3.pdf)

## Method choices and dependencies

| Capability and fidelity | First selected approach | Alternatives and qualitative cost | Evidence/data gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material verification | Existing synthetic isotropic power law | Negligible evaluation cost; suitable for exact integrals and history tests | Never label a synthetic coefficient set as calibrated |
| Bounded physical flow stress | Measured curves or a parsimonious fitted law indexed by material state, rate and temperature | Tabulation is cheap and transparent; richer empirical fits remain cheap but extrapolate differently | Independent loading curves and unit/strain conventions; parameter covariance and held-out conditions |
| Evolving hardening | One independently identified internal-state model when simple curves fail | Kocks–Mecking-type evolution is inexpensive per point; coupled precipitate models need much more calibration; crystal plasticity is substantially more expensive | Microstructure or loading-history observations that distinguish competing mechanisms |
| Thermal ledger | Enthalpy-based lumped control volumes with declared sources and boundaries | Cheap; useful for conservation and total-energy checks, insufficient for gradients | Positive heat capacity, heat-source units, passivity, boundedness and temporal refinement |
| Strip/roll temperature | One-dimensional through-thickness strip and appropriately resolved roll model | Moderate cost; reduced FE if gradients or history warrant it; 2D/3D thermo-mechanical FE costs much more | Measured temperatures and boundary-condition identifiability independent of force fitting |
| Thermal-mechanical feedback | Partitioned iteration with a convergence contract inside a verified weak-feedback domain | Moderate cost; fully coupled nonlinear solve if splitting error or strong softening warrants it | Convergence under iteration/time/space refinement and independent force plus temperature comparisons |
| Interpass evolution | Prescribed measured temperature histories first; then identified cooling and anneal kinetics | Lumped cooling is cheap; coil conduction and heterogeneous annealing need spatial resolution and longer histories | Dwell time, geometry, fluid/environment conditions, initial thermal field and anneal observations |
| Friction/lubrication | Calibrated bounded friction law with explicit contact regime | Mixed lubrication adds roughness and entrainment state; thermohydrodynamics adds substantial nonlinear cost | Force, torque, slip and film/roughness evidence jointly separate friction from flow stress |
| Anisotropy/residual state | Scalar model only for outputs its validation supports; retain extension interfaces | 3D phenomenological plasticity is moderate; texture evolution/crystal plasticity high cost | Directional tests and unloading/exit-stress observations; no flatness claim from mean force |
| Damage/surface quality | Record observables and constraints without predicted failure margins | Calibrated local damage then nonlocal/gradient models if localisation matters; mesh resolution can dominate cost | Relevant alloy/temper stress paths, fracture/surface observations and length-scale convergence |

Material state may include accumulated plastic strain, internal hardening variables, temperature, texture, grain size, recrystallised fraction, precipitate state and damage when supported. Equipment owns roll geometry, elastic/thermal properties, coolant-delivery capabilities and rated envelopes. Pass controls own target reduction, speed, applied tension and coolant settings; measured boundary histories carry timestamped provenance. This separation allows the next pass to inherit state while changing control settings and equipment-dependent constraints.

All future equation adapters must declare force versus stress, signed tension convention, mean versus local flow stress, roll radius versus diameter, torque per roll versus total mill torque, heat flux versus power, material volume versus mass and strain definition. A successful force fit cannot resolve all of those conventions: compensating errors can produce the same force. Source-specific ambiguity is an exclusion condition for quantitative acceptance, even when a qualitative comparison remains informative.

These candidates contain measured evidence or useful verification definitions. None has been established here as a complete, openly licensed machine-readable rolling dataset with raw replicates and uncertainty metadata. Article accessibility and data redistribution rights are separate questions.

| R053 cooling rig | Thermocouple-based inverse cooling response | Method verification; steel substrate and apparatus preclude direct aluminium coefficient transfer |

| R091 thermometry study | Spectral emissivity and temperature-estimation comparisons | Instrument-model challenge; temperature and surface domains differ from a cold lubricated strip |
| R052/R058–R060 manuals | Governing-equation examples and implementation conventions | Numerical verification references only; no measured plant performance |

Digitising a graph creates a derived dataset that needs pixel/calibration uncertainty, units, figure/page provenance and an explicit extraction method. Repeated points from a plotted fitted curve are not independent measurements. Keep calibration, model selection and final holdout data separate at the specimen, coil or process-condition level, so near-duplicate passes cannot inflate apparent generalisation.

## Staged research backlog and acceptance gates

1. **Freeze one benchmark package and reconcile conventions.** Select an accessible measured case, retain raw reported values and their provenance, distinguish digitised graphs from tables, and document all transformations. The separate AA8015 thesis review is a promising force/flow-curve route; R049 supplies an additional industrial-force comparison with explicit thermal caveats. R062 remains conditional on radius, strain, friction and velocity interpretation. Accept only when a second reviewer reproduces the dimensional transformations and the comparison variables have unambiguous definitions. Stop quantitative use of unresolved inputs.
2. **Identify the minimum material model.** Obtain alloy/temper/composition, prior processing, true stress–plastic strain curves, orientation, strain-rate and temperature coverage with replicate/scatter information. Fit the simplest justified law and preserve alternatives when the data cannot distinguish them. Hold out curves or processing states before selecting the model. Acceptance requires residuals and predictive intervals that meet output-specific tolerances agreed before fitting; do not invent a universal percentage. Stop extrapolation when required states leave supported coverage.
3. **Close the thermal verification gaps.** Resolve the parent-reported large-step midpoint contact overshoot and global entropy issue before relying on passive contact at arbitrary steps. Test energy conservation separately from nonnegative dissipation, temperature bounds and time convergence, including unequal/temperature-dependent heat capacities. Independent analytic limits and numerical reference integrations are needed. Passing an energy residual alone is insufficient.
4. **Validate temperature and friction separately enough to identify them.** Collect synchronized force, per-roll torque where available, forward slip, inlet/outlet gauge and temperature, speed, tension, roughness and coolant state. Include instrument calibration and uncertainty. Use a designed variation of speed, reduction and coolant conditions to expose parameter correlations; fitting force alone cannot uniquely identify friction and strength. R053 is a cooling-method benchmark, not an aluminium coefficient source. R091 is a thermometry-method benchmark, not a cold-strip calibration.
5. **Add bounded temperature/rate feedback.** Compare sequential, iterated and, if needed, fully coupled solutions on the same measured cases. Track source terms and thermo-mechanical convergence. Accept a fidelity tier only inside the range where its omitted effects lie within the chosen output uncertainty budget. Stop if a predicted improvement merely transfers mismatch between force and temperature.
6. **Add interpass cooling and microstructure only when observable.** Begin with measured temperature/time histories, then identify heat-transfer and recovery/anneal submodels independently. Validate new dwell/heating histories and carry internal state consistently into the next mechanical pass. Do not replace an unknown anneal with an undocumented strain reset. Precipitation and texture evolution can remain deferred if the selected alloy/process and required outputs do not justify them.
7. **Enable schedule comparison and advisory outputs.** Propagate material, friction, thermal and measurement uncertainty through the supported pass range. Publish constraint violations and out-of-domain flags alongside objective values. A schedule optimiser must stop rather than exploit extrapolated softening, arbitrary anneal resets or unknown failure margins. Quality, residual stress and damage predictions need their own gates; mean-force accuracy is insufficient.

Ordinary engineering work includes typed material-card schemas, dataset manifests, citation/version capture, reproducible fitting scripts, test fixtures, plots and UI uncertainty displays. Required science gaps include material identification, friction/flow-stress separation, thermal boundary calibration, anneal kinetics and damage/quality observability. More software cannot substitute for those observations.

The near-term deployment contribution is a local-first offline advisory package with versioned material cards, source-linked assumptions, reproducible runs, explicit validity ranges and a record of changed settings at every pass. Edge or on-prem advisory integration needs data contracts, sensor alignment, access controls and monitoring of domain drift. Live closed-loop control remains a separate safety/controls programme and authorization decision; these reviewed sources do not authorize autonomous plant operation.

## Access, data and expert requests

The parent should resolve the first target alloy/temper/product family, required outputs and acceptable measured validation errors with the user. Laboratory or plant contributors must specify which load, torque, slip, temperature and microstructure records can lawfully be shared. Material and tribology specialists should review parameter identifiability and loading-mode transfer; a thermal specialist should review contact partition and the passive-contact defect; annealing and quality specialists are needed before those predictions become constraints.

Priority lawful access requests are R050's exact manuscript, R068's full annealing article and author list, R065–R067's constitutive details, R073–R074's relevant rolling damage evidence, and R089's integration algorithm. R051 has a separately accessible newer thermal-contact manual for conceptual cross-checking, but its unavailable 2024 page remains a ledger gap. The whole 37-record set, including inaccessible items, stays in the corpus; source count alone does not establish adequacy.
