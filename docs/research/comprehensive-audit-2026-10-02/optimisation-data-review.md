# Optimisation, learning, measured data and validation review

Audit date: 2026-10-02. Implementation inspected at `caec37ef86a8b78e1eb88292ea6368bfb64bcbdc`. This review covers original bibliography R023–R035 and seven related or newly identified records. It contributes to the comprehensive project audit; it is not the whole-library count or a deployment approval.

## Findings that change the plan

The next scientific milestone should establish a small, independently measured aluminium benchmark with identifiable material, friction and machine parameters. A wider search algorithm cannot compensate for uncertain forward physics. The existing `optimise_gauges` function already performs a narrowly bounded, conditional three-pass mesh search; describing the project as having no optimisation at all would be inaccurate. It does not yet provide a general validated route planner.

The reviewed literature supports keeping machine compliance, material response, friction, thermal history and learned residuals separately identifiable and versioned. It does not establish that a neural network or evolutionary optimiser is necessary for the current problem size. Begin with reproducible forward replay and deterministic low-dimensional search. Add learned correction or surrogate acceleration only after a measured validation protocol shows a benefit against that baseline.

Choi 2026 is useful for the structure of an aggregate energy optimisation problem. Its reported model-based improvements do not establish prospective production savings. The supporting file's tension semantics remain unresolved; no Choi tension bound, material card, energy efficiency or claimed saving should enter project defaults. The original published paper was successfully reread in this audit; its separately linked DOCX supplement was not. Those access outcomes must not be conflated.

## Competing approaches and recommended sequence

The following selections are project recommendations derived from the audit, rather than claims that a source proves one universally best algorithm.

| Approach | Appropriate use and cost | Dependencies and reasons to defer |
|---|---|---|
| Deterministic forward simulation plus enumeration, mesh or bounded direct search | First choice for a small fixed-pass problem. Cheap to moderate; transparent constraint failures and sensitivity. | Requires consistent state propagation and measured or declared material/friction inputs. Search resolution and unsearched regions must be disclosed. |
| Gradient-based constrained optimisation | Efficient for sufficiently smooth calibrated models and many continuous settings. | Contact-mode changes, infeasible solvers and numerical noise require derivative checks, multiple starts and independent replay. A learned residual's derivative may be poor despite small force error. |
| Mixed discrete/continuous route planning | Enumerate a defensible small set of pass counts, process stages or anneal choices, then solve continuous settings. | Requires validated transition models, handling-time/cooling/anneal data and machine-specific constraints; variable pass count is not merely another continuous gauge variable. |
| NSGA-II, differential evolution or other population search | Useful for non-smooth, multiple-objective tradeoffs when evaluations are affordable. Usually many more forward evaluations. | Seed, population and stopping criteria influence results. Compare against simple baselines at matched evaluation budgets; no inspected paper establishes universal superiority. |
| Surrogate-assisted search | Reduce cost when a trusted high-fidelity model is demonstrably the bottleneck. | Must distinguish emulator error from physical model discrepancy. Reject or verify outside-domain queries and independently replay all retained candidates. A surrogate of synthetic data remains synthetic evidence. |
| Physics-guided residual or parameter-learning model | Consider after a reproducible bias is identified with adequate measured coverage. | Estimate identifiable parameters first; bound residuals and test them by alloy, temper, coil, time and operating regime. Learned flexibility can hide erroneous units, histories or machine parameters. |
| PDE-residual PINN | Potentially useful when abundant forward queries or inverse problems amortize training cost. | For the present low-dimensional slab problem, compare wall time and accuracy with direct solvers before adopting training complexity. One slip observation cannot identify an arbitrary friction field. |
| Full-field FE, roll-stack and contact/thermal coupling | Offline high-fidelity studies and targeted benchmark generation. Highest setup/calibration/compute burden. | Constitutive, contact and mesh uncertainty remain; convergence alone is not validation. Reduced models can follow, with their own error envelope. |

Keep objectives physically distinct: time, electrical energy, force/power margin, dimensional quality, surface quality and risk. Some may be hard constraints rather than objectives. If a carbon or cost objective is a fixed multiplier of energy, disclose that it contributes no independent tradeoff under those assumptions. Avoid an opaque weighted score until stakeholders establish meaningful priorities; retain the Pareto set and reasons for discarding candidates.

A robust planner must cover epistemic uncertainties (model choice, unmeasured heat transfer, parameter ambiguity) as well as run-to-run variation. Start with documented scenario envelopes and sensitivity analysis. Chance constraints require defensible distributions and dependence estimates; they should not be attached to invented distributions. Marginal historical minima and maxima do not guarantee that their combinations are feasible. Candidate verification must use the original solver, state/history propagation and all constraints, with numerical tolerances smaller than the claimed improvement.

## Data, identifiability and validation protocol

The best public measured-data ingestion lead in this assigned corpus is R034, but it is a steel surface-roughness dataset, not a general aluminium benchmark. R027's public labels are simulations. Choi's paper describes industrial aggregate energy but this audit did not recover a reusable raw plant dataset or licence for its supplement. R023 and R024 provide useful empirical publication evidence without an independently audited reusable measurement archive. None supplies a verified universal aluminium material card or equipment-limit table.

For each acquired dataset, store provenance, immutable version/checksum, file-specific licence, measured/simulated/derived label, alloy/composition, temper and history, geometry, sensor calibration, acquisition interval, units, missing-value meanings, filtering and exclusions. Preserve raw evidence outside public git if redistribution is not authorized; commit only permitted fixtures, manifests and original analysis. For simulation data also store constitutive/contact versions, mesh, time step, mass scaling, convergence studies and energy balance. A simulated validation set tests an emulator; it does not establish plant prediction accuracy.

Calibration should progress in separable stages:

1. Measure geometry and machine quantities independently where possible: roll diameter, strip width/gauge, speed, force-channel calibration, drive/reel conventions and compliance. Bound uncertainty rather than folding it into a fitted friction coefficient.
2. Establish alloy/temper-specific flow response over the intended strain, strain-rate and temperature domain. Accumulated true strain, equivalent plastic strain and reduction ratio require explicit conversion; do not fit them interchangeably.
3. Estimate an effective friction/contact model using multiple observables and operating conditions. Force alone can trade off against material strength; slip and torque may help but also require independent measurement and correct geometry. Use sensitivity rank, correlated parameter intervals and profile likelihood or equivalent diagnostics to identify unresolved combinations.
4. Calibrate thermal terms against measured strip/roll temperatures and relevant heat-transfer observations. Electrical energy includes drive losses, idle time and auxiliaries; daily totals alone cannot uniquely identify all local thermomechanical parameters.
5. Only then evaluate a constrained learned correction. Reserve a final untouched test set and document why its domain supports each intended use.

Split data by coil/heat/route and time before learned preprocessing or hyperparameter selection. Keep connected passes and near-duplicate simulation states in one group. Include later-day and held-alloy/temper tests where those are claimed deployment domains. A random row split can overstate generalisation; it is not sufficient evidence of leakage by itself, so record the actual provenance before diagnosing it.

Report errors in physical units, signed bias and tails by operating regime; include uncertainty coverage and failure/abstention rates. Separate repeatability, sensor uncertainty, calibration error, numerical error, emulator error and model discrepancy. Benchmark all contenders on identical data splits and evaluation budgets. Compare with constant/physical baselines and current code. Include mass conservation, limiting cases, sign changes, contact/neutral-point admissibility, mesh and time-step convergence, and thermodynamic consistency as appropriate. These checks catch defects but cannot replace independent observations.

## Priorities, decision gates and deployment endpoint

| Priority and type | Actionable deliverable | Acceptance gate or stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| P0 — evidence/science | Resolve Shao benchmark assumptions through the independent benchmark review; obtain original R025 geometry and Choi tension clarification. | Quarantined values remain excluded unless dimensional interpretation and provenance are explicit. A failed clarification does not block work on other defensible benchmarks. |
| P0 — science | Choose a tractable measured aluminium case with alloy/temper, force, gauges, speeds, tensions and roll geometry; document missing quantities. | If material/contact parameters cannot be separated, report a conditional reproduction and identifiable combinations, not calibrated predictive validation. |
| P0 — engineering | Add a versioned evidence/data manifest and grouped split specification; pin public data versions and verify licences/files. | No data import accepted solely from a README or unresolved moving branch. The present ledger deliberately records those limitations. |
| P1 — science | Calibrated low-fidelity forward benchmark, uncertainty budget and held-route evaluation. | Predeclare tolerances from measurement uncertainty and intended decision sensitivity; do not invent universal acceptance percentages. Stop domain expansion when residuals or uncertainty exceed that budget. |
| P1 — engineering/science | Extend the existing bounded search only within a validated domain, with independent candidate replay and feasible-baseline comparison. | No claimed improvement smaller than the propagated numerical/model uncertainty; no candidate accepted with a failed solver or unverified machine constraint. |
| P2 — science | Add rate/thermal/history and foil/pack physics in dependency order where the chosen use case requires them. | Each extension gets its own measured benchmark and transfer test; a broad optimiser must not conceal missing transition physics. |
| P2 — engineering | Scenario UI, explanatory plots and optional 3D view using the same versioned result contract. | Display conditional inputs, domain, uncertainty and rejection reason. A 3D animation is not additional physical validation. |
| P3 — engineering | Reproducible local-first offline/advisory package with pinned dependencies, data/model versions, run manifests, regression fixtures and exportable audit trail. | Install/reproduce on a clean target; protect confidential measurements; explicit identity/access and signed or checked releases where needed. No control-channel write access is necessary. |
| P4 — engineering/science | On-prem or edge advisory pilot, initially replay then shadow mode, with operator review and measured post-run comparison. | Agree latency, ownership, retention, backup, rollback, drift detection and abstention thresholds. Diagnose changes before updating model versions. |
| Separate programme | Any eventual live industrial closed-loop control. | Requires controls/safety experts, plant authorization, hazard analysis, interlocks, fail-safe behaviour, independent validation and an approved management-of-change process. The research roadmap does not authorize equipment operation. |

A sensible current endpoint is a local-first advisory simulator: reproducible offline runs, interpretable route alternatives, recorded evidence assumptions, validated applicability envelopes and human selection. Edge/on-prem services become options after data ownership, target hardware, users and latency are agreed. Root should ask the user for material decisions when those choices affect scope: target alloy/product/temper and mill, available measured records, primary quality constraints, deployment users, and access/licensing support. Progress now is possible on audit closure, conditional benchmark replay, data schemas, calibration diagnostics and software verification; plant credentials and live connections are not prerequisites.

This review does not recommend training a large model, procuring compute, changing equipment settings or implementing closed-loop control. Its selected path gives a testable scientific reason for each later engineering phase.
