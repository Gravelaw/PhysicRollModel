## Numerical verification: stability, bounds and the actual thermal defect

R160's scalar decay equation has theta-method amplification

`A = [1 − (1−theta)*a*dt] / [1 + theta*a*dt]`.

For positive `a`, bounded magnitude and nonnegative amplification are distinct requirements. With centred integration, `|A| <= 1` still permits sign alternation when `a*dt > 2`; backward Euler is monotone for this scalar problem. These conclusions are directly grounded in publisher pp.44–46, Eqs.2.3–2.6. They do not imply that every A-stable or L-stable nonlinear coupled method preserves every physical bound. Accuracy still needs refinement. [R160, chapter 2](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-319-29439-1_2)

The project's [I01 finding](implementation-traceability.md) requires a more careful interpretation than simply calling the API a closed two-body ODE. In [energy_handoff.py](../../../physicroll/energy_handoff.py), the strip equation uses steady enthalpy transport with mass flow; the roll equation uses a finite mass over a time interval. Their arithmetic midpoints combine a spatial/representative strip average and a temporal roll average. By contrast, [thermal.py](../../../physicroll/thermal.py) is a standalone two-capacity Euler step with its own step restriction. They require different verification fixtures.

The following equations are **project-specific algebra derived from the inspected closure**, not equations quoted from R160. With constant heat capacities, zero external sources, `Cs = mdot*cp_s`, `Cr = Mroll*cp_r/dt`, and `DeltaT = Tr,in−Ts,in`, the current law yields

`Q = G*DeltaT / [1 + (G/2)*(1/Cs + 1/Cr)]`.

Both `Cs` and `Cr` here have units W/K. In the nearly fixed-roll limit `Cr → infinity`,

`(Ts,out−Ts,in)/DeltaT = r/(1+r/2)`, where `r = G/Cs`.

This ratio exceeds one when `r > 2`, and tends to two. Thus a strip can overshoot the reservoir even when the roll barely changes temperature. Reducing the temporal interval increases `Cr`; it does not decrease `G/Cs`. Time substepping alone cannot close the full admissibility gap. This agrees with the independent thermal review and avoids transferring a batch-ODE stability bound to a flowing-stream closure.

For a future explicitly defined plug-flow, fixed-reservoir, constant-property reference with uniformly distributed conductance, the exact outlet relation is `Ts,out = Tr + (Ts,in−Tr)*exp(−G/Cs)`. This is a proposed analytic fixture under those assumptions, not a measured rolling law or an automatic replacement for the current API. Define transport geometry and conductance allocation before comparing implementations to it.

P1 must separately demonstrate: conservative energy accounting; physically bounded pure exchange for both hot-strip and hot-roll directions; whole-process entropy accounting using `integral cp(T)/T dT`; temporal and material-coordinate refinement; and correct limits of zero conductance, large roll capacity, weak exchange and unequal capacities. The present bisection residual only verifies the stated algebraic contact equation. A tighter root tolerance will not repair an inadmissible closure. Do not clip an overshoot without rechecking energy and entropy.

## Conditional inverse calibration: what a fitted value means

Hansen’s reviewed teaching material (R163) shows how weakly observable solution directions amplify measurement perturbations and why regularisation adds bias while suppressing noise. The exposed chapter 5 introduction also makes parameter-choice assumptions explicit. A low fitting residual alone cannot certify accurate hidden parameters. [R163, author teaching collection](https://people.compute.dtu.dk/pcha/DIPbook.html); [R161, official excerpts](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/book/10.1137/1.9780898718836)

The P2 proposal should therefore remain the narrowly specified one in the [roadmap](roadmap.md): freeze the Shao source adapter and fit at most an identifiable effective initial-hardening parameter to the selected calibration observation. Report a no-fit reference, the fitting interval, sensitivity, boundary solutions and all other force/slip residuals. This fitted quantity is not a measured incoming prestrain. Three correlated passes cannot justify jointly identifying material hardening, initial history, friction, geometry and machine compliance while their source meanings remain unsettled.

A useful diagnostic is a dimensionless, observation-scaled sensitivity matrix: perturb each candidate parameter on its declared scale, evaluate output changes at converged solver settings, and inspect its singular spectrum and parameter correlations. Verify finite-difference perturbations against numerical error. If columns are nearly dependent, retain only identifiable combinations or request independent material, slip, torque or geometry measurements. A numerically full-rank local matrix is not proof of global uniqueness. Regularisation does not create missing evidence; disclose its strength and interpretation.

Synthetic recovery tests can establish that an inference implementation returns known inputs in a declared experiment. They are insufficient if generated and inverted with precisely the same model, discretisation and assumptions. Include deliberate parameter perturbations and independent forward/reference calculations; later compare with measurements reserved by coil or campaign. Since all three published Shao passes have already been inspected, later-pass comparisons are not pristine blind holdouts. No new confidence interval should be assigned without a defensible observation-error model.

## Optimisation and verification claims must be separate

R159 supplies explicit convexity and optimality assumptions, including KKT conditions and regularised fitting formulations. Those guarantees apply only when their conditions hold. [R159, selected full-text sections](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf)

The current [literature.py](../../../physicroll/literature.py) search evaluates two intermediate gauges in a conditional three-pass case. Its retained `global_optimum_proven=False` is appropriate. A dense grid, a local minimum or a small optimisation residual establishes neither global optimality nor experimentally correct forward predictions. Keep baseline and candidate numerical refinement, explicit constraint rejection and source-assumption manifests. Extend to robust or multiobjective planning after the calibrated domain and objective uncertainties are known. If a future subproblem is proven convex, use that structure and an appropriate certificate; do not assume the full rolling route is convex because a convex solver can be called.

R162's official summaries distinguish checking the numerical algorithm/implementation from merely matching a remembered output, and point to exact-solution/order testing. Full chapters remain unread. Their presence in the TOC is a reading plan, not evidence that this project already follows all procedures. [R162, code verification summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/verification-and-validation-in-scientific-computing/code-verification/733E362E9B977EA1E3745BF9FAF607A0)
