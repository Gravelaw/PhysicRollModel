# Per-pass operational phases, model validity and dynamic simulation

Research supplement, 2 October 2026. Implementation baseline remains `caec37ef86a8b78e1eb88292ea6368bfb64bcbdc`. This is a research/data/architecture requirement for the core simulator, not a late animation feature or an instruction to operate a mill.

## Requirement provenance and what external evidence establishes

The user reports that a pass ramps up from startup, some first-principles models become active, established rolling follows, and the remaining coil length triggers deceleration. Recipes and actual values vary by alloy, gauge and pass stage. Record this as **UR-OPS-01**, supplied via the parent from user message `userSentinel_6f1ad09d73e8819183b4e92d66e43c59`. It motivates the requirements below; it does not establish a universal activation sequence. External research proceeds without requiring the user to name models or provide a recipe.

Sources support a richer lifecycle. ABB describes nonlinear simulation across complete passes, including acceleration and deceleration (R164, printed p47). Its later controls document explicitly distinguishes threading, tail-out and reversing, transport-delayed gauge feedback, and speed feed-forward during acceleration/deceleration (R165, PDF pp2–3). The original slowdown patent describes a threading→acceleration→run→slowdown sequence and a gauge/speed-dependent payoff-diameter trigger (R171, description and Fig1). These establish examples of practice, not this project's mill configuration or numerical limits. [ABB 2004](https://library.e.abb.com/public/76bffa60a31d625cc1256f57004df84c/44-49%204M891.pdf), [ABB controls](https://library.e.abb.com/public/04580d2383cc4b53b8573d1bb8eda2e8/Technological_Controls_for_Cold_Rolling_Mill_190815_LowRes.pdf), [US3518857A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3518857A/en).

Different models have different activation rules. ABB's setup calculations can occur **before threading**, its thermal-roll model can continue during and after rolling, and measured-data adaptation is a separate function (R112, pp46–48). Wehr's laboratory steel study starts adaptation only after valid synchronized measurements become available; its mill-modulus estimator also requires operation in the identified linear load range and assumes parameter changes are slow relative to measurement transport delay (R026, §§4,6.3). Thus 'turn on first principles after ramp-up' must be decomposed by model, output and data availability. No reviewed source establishes one speed threshold suitable for every model/alloy/pass. [ABB setup models](https://library.e.abb.com/public/77efb4c9677e46c5c125759100274942/43-48%201M924_ENG72dpi.pdf), [Wehr et al.](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/817497/files/817497.pdf).

Primetals' aluminium automation documentation confirms the practical distinction between setup information, drive speed encoders and coiler tension control (R166). Its coil-eccentricity page discusses acceleration/deceleration and winding resonances/torque constraints in a **steel** application (R168). Neither supplies this project's control settings, plant-safe limits or an aluminium transient benchmark. [Aluminium automation](https://www.primetals.com/en/portfolio/solutions/cold-rolling/non-ferrous/aluminum/automation-and-modernization-for-aluminum-cold-rolling-mills/), [coil eccentricity](https://www.primetals.com/en/portfolio/solutions/cold-rolling/coil-eccentricity-expert/).

See [primary operational-paper review](operational-papers-review.md) for the distinction between coupled electromechanical rolling dynamics, sequential stand-state FE transfers and empirical speed compensation. Paper-specific tests and steel results are not silently transferred to aluminium. ## Proposed operational state machine

The following is a **project design proposal informed by those sources**, not a recovered plant PLC programme. Store operation phase independently from material state, equipment configuration, actuator/control mode, recipe and model-validity status. Different plants can omit or split phases; continuous tandem, reversing, batch, foil and pack routes require explicit topology. Tension availability may differ by side and stand during threading/tail-out.

| State | Entry/exit evidence to record | Recipe and actual behaviour | Model implications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepared / stationary | Coil/pass identity, loaded recipe revision, material and roll state, confirmed direction; next event records provenance | Target route, phase-specific ranges, expected head/tail treatment; actual installed configuration separately | Setup prediction may run with declared assumptions; no positive-speed steady bite prediction at rest |
| Threading / bite establishment | Strip presence, bite/load evidence, reel engagement, clamp/pinch status if measured; incomplete sides explicitly unknown | Thread speed and gap/tension targets differ from normal rolling; track actual force, speed, slip and tension availability | Current fully established steady solver may be inapplicable; record events without inventing transient force or zero-tension completion |
| Ramp-up / tension establishment | Actual speed trajectory and acceleration, established tension sides, tracking status and controller mode; interrupted ramps retained | Phase/alloy/temper/gauge/pass-specific targets, ramps and trim history; recipe values never replace measurements | Only qualified quasi-steady samples may be evaluated; dynamic drive/span/thermal responses require additional models |
| Settled rolling | Declared dwell plus accepted tracking, sensor quality and output-specific stationarity; transitions can be revoked | Target versus actual bands, drift and deviations remain visible | Steady mechanics eligible only inside its domain; adaptation has its own identification/data gates, not automatic permission from this label |
| Length-triggered deceleration | Remaining-length/diameter estimate, estimator version/uncertainty, trigger source and time, actual speed change | Deceleration/tension targets may differ from ramp-up; braking/regeneration and duty limits matter | Reassess quasi-steady eligibility and parameter updates; explicitly preserve thermal and material history |
| Tail-out / strip release | Tail location and loss of each span/tension/contact path, final length and crop events | End treatment, traction loss and changing topology are recorded | Disable outputs whose boundary conditions no longer exist; do not reuse settled tensions through release |
| Reversal / next pass / coil complete | Actual zero crossing, reel-role and direction change, new pass ID, gauge/history handoff | New pass recipe and actual initial states; no implicit anneal or history reset | Transform directional observation definitions explicitly, preserve material genealogy; current positive-speed solver needs a declared oriented-pass adapter |
| Interrupted / unavailable | Stop, hold, abort, invalid data, lost tracking or model-domain failure with reasons | No automatic extrapolation across unknown intervals; retained recipe is marked stale where necessary | Suspend unsupported advice/adaptation; re-entry requires re-established tracking, valid state and new eligibility evidence |

Operational phase is an observation/inference with confidence and provenance. A command to accelerate is not proof of actual acceleration; a reached speed setpoint is not proof all thermal, mechanical or measurement fields are settled. R014 demonstrates within its FE setting that steady force/torque can precede steady internal fields (§4.4, Figure 6 of the 2024 preprint v1); it does not prescribe a plant dwell time. [Flanagan et al., preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03242v1).

## Existing implementation boundary

The baseline already has phase tags `threading`, `steady`, `deceleration` and `tailout` in `operating.py`, per-phase supplied ranges/observations, and `NOT_EVALUATED_PHASE` for nonsteady operating checks. `sensors.py` exposes material-coordinate/timestamp/quality fields with unavailable values where data are absent. `observations.reel_shaft_torque_nm` can account for a supplied `I*alpha` term, while `equipment.evaluate_equipment` uses `alpha=0` in its snapshots. These are useful starting contracts. They do not integrate reel/spanning-strip dynamics, reconstruct event transitions, predict remaining length, implement model-enable logic or validate ramps. O0 should extend these structures; it should not erase their conservative nonsteady status.

## Three fidelity levels and their boundaries

**O0 — event and observation replay.** Reconstruct phases, recipe versions, measured trajectories and material coordinates with uncertainty; show what happened and what is missing. This can be designed before dynamic physics. Historical replay is not a controller, and measured output traces are not simulated predictions.

**O1 — phase-qualified quasi-steady evaluation.** Sample identified, sufficiently settled operating points with contemporaneous material/contact conditions; compare the existing solver to aligned observations. Eligibility needs output-specific timescale evidence and error budgets. Acceleration samples can be labelled exploratory scenarios but cannot claim inertial tension, drive power, transient gauge response or full-pass quality. Never count successive time samples of different strip parcels as repeated plastic passes on the same material state. Do not divide work/heat or accumulate strain merely because a sampling rate changes.

**O2 — explicit coupled dynamic simulator.** Add material transport, changing reel geometry/inertia, drive and actuator dynamics, span tension/elasticity, contact and thermal/history evolution, observation delays and discrete mode changes. A steady bite model can be a qualified algebraic component, but replacing its speed by a ramp does not create O2. Alternative implementations include reduced ODE/DAE plus algebraic bite models, segmented transport, or coupled FE where local transient fields justify the cost. Select the smallest identifiable model that answers the output need; resolve stiffness, event handling, time/spatial convergence and conservation before using it for scheduling.

Existing plant control algorithms are external systems whose modes, versions and signals may later be observed. They are not automatically replicated by O2. Controller design, hardware-in-loop, site integration and live closed-loop authority remain a separate programme even after an offline dynamic simulator is validated.

## Physical and data contracts needed before O2

1. **Material-coordinate tracking.** Integrate located measured transport speed with timestamps and uncertainty, retaining direction and missed encoder counts. Align entry/exit samples through material travel, not a fixed delay during acceleration. Use conserved mass/volume or a stated reference coordinate across reduction, width changes, crops and reversals. Remaining length includes unreduced input geometry, wound layers, threading path and estimator uncertainty. A uniform tightly packed coil's geometric estimate (using its actual wound gauge) `L = pi*(D^2-Dcore^2)/(4*h)` is only a proposed sanity check under those assumptions, not an exact estimator for variable gauge, loose winding or uncertain diameter.
2. **Reels and drives.** Distinguish strip tension force, shaft torque, motor torque, gear ratio, angular velocity, power and both-roll versus single-roll scope. For a declared direction and no slip at the winding contact, with `v` the located line speed there and `R` the instantaneous outer coil contact radius, `omega=v/R` implies `alpha=(dv/dt)/R-v*(dR/dt)/R^2`. A winding control volume needs correct incoming/outgoing angular-momentum flux; blindly applying a closed-body `d(J*omega)/dt` to changing coil mass can double-count terms. Obtain the chosen derivation and measured inertias/losses before implementation. ABB R045 explicitly separates mechanical-loss and inertia compensation and identifies diameter/material inputs; R172 documents alternative sensed/estimated tension and length sources. Their vendor parameters are not recommended project values. [DCS800 technical module](https://library.e.abb.com/public/fcab7666a8494f228dbdf7f0074e35dc/G563e%20%20Part%202%20DCS800-Winder%20WINDER_INDIR_02R0101.pdf), [ACS880 Rev D](https://library.e.abb.com/public/b103c5c12ec749edb727e684d3a394eb/AWIL140x_en.pdf).
3. **Rate, friction and thermal history.** Changing speed changes residence and strain-rate scales, lubrication conditions and available cooling time. Which effects are material depends on alloy/temper, gauge, pass and lubricant. Current prescribed friction and temperature-independent flow law cannot establish these dynamics. Preserve roll thermal history during stops and interpass delays; separate advected strip parcels from finite roll storage. I01's numerical correction does not supply a cooling law, rate law or transient lubrication law.
4. **Recipe versus measurement.** Store approved recipe ID/version, material and product applicability, pass and phase, target trajectory/ranges, transition conditions and source. Separately store operator trims, applied setpoints with command/acknowledgement times, actuator/controller modes, measured values with quality/uncertainty, and predicted values with model versions. Track signed errors against the contemporaneous applied reference and product requirement; do not backfill unknown measurements with commands. Bounds need type (equipment, process, material, quality, model domain), validity interval, source and applicability.
5. **Observation provenance.** Need synchronized raw encoders, reel/roll speeds and diameters, entry/exit tensions, force, gap/position, motor current/torque/power, gauges, coolant and temperature observations; sensor locations/bandwidth/filtering, timebase and delays; head/tail/bite/engagement/mode bits; recipe/trim/limit histories and interruption records. Thermal/film/flatness outputs need their own sensors. Sampling rate must resolve the proposed dynamics; no universal Hz requirement is invented.

## Separate model eligibility, adaptation and fallback

For each model/output maintain `not_applicable`, `waiting_for_data`, `eligible_unvalidated`, `qualified_for_domain`, `degraded` or `invalid`, plus reason, evidence window, data/model/recipe versions and phase. These are proposed semantic states, not new code. Eligibility cannot promote an uncalibrated model to validated status.

The offline eligibility record should require: established relevant boundary conditions; finite compatible SI values; valid sensor alignment and age; material/geometry/contact domain; numerical convergence/conservation; and output-specific transient qualification. Adaptation additionally requires an identifiable parameter subset, appropriate load/excitation domain, accepted data quality, prior/covariance/version and rejection/update limits. Missing sensors, load below an identified range, rapid parameter change compared with transport delay, saturation, slip/chatter, phase/topology change or broken provenance can suspend updating even when a nominal prediction remains possible. These are proposed review gates whose thresholds need evidence; they are not claims that every cited controller implements them.

Fallback for this advisory project means retaining labelled observations, reverting to a previously qualified offline model where its domain still applies, or suppressing an unsupported recommendation. A prior model is not automatically safe outside its domain. Re-entry after interruption requires a fresh coordinate/coil/recipe reconciliation, restored input quality, consistent carried state and a qualified transition window. Do not silently resume a previous adaptation state or reuse data across changed rolls/alloys/directions. No fallback sends equipment commands.

## Validation matrix and acceptance gates

| Scenario | Required comparison/check | Failure or stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Normal head→ramp→settled→tail on one identified pass | Compare independently labelled transitions, setpoint/actual lag, force/tension/gauge trajectories and material positions | Unknown labels remain unknown; reject false settled classification or missing boundary conditions |
| Same product at different commanded ramps; different alloy/gauge/pass at similar speed | Grouped campaign validation; separate dynamic bias from material/friction parameter error | No universal speed-only eligibility or pooled accuracy that hides head/tail errors |
| Short coil with no settled interval | Correctly retain ramp/deceleration-only history; no fabricated steady window | Do not score a nonexistent plateau or use steady-only calibration as full-pass validation |
| Length/diameter error, encoder slip or missing samples | Track uncertainty, test estimator disagreement and event-time sensitivity | No deterministic remaining-length claim or automatic trigger inferred from unreliable data |
| Direction reversal and changing reel roles | Signed mass/length/torque/power, mapping of tension sides and parcel genealogy; energy including storage | No negative physical thickness, duplicated material, lost history or arbitrary absolute-value fixes |
| Stop/restart, tail tension loss, sensor dropout or actuator saturation | Explicit mode changes, bounded interpolation policy, frozen adaptation and documented re-entry | No bridging unknown intervals with fabricated data or unsupported prediction |
| Dynamic numerical limits | Steady limit, fixed-radius/inertia limit, free response and signed regenerative power; time/spatial refinement; event-local conservation | Repeated steady solves cannot pass as dynamic verification; wrong variable-mass balance blocks O2 |
| Model adaptation enabled/disabled | Compare frozen-model baseline and qualified updates on aligned independent coils; audit parameter identifiability and update events | No learning from future samples, unsupported extrapolation or hidden compensation for unit errors |
| Head/steady/tail quality and energy | Report per-phase errors/coverage and length-weighted product metrics; integrate defined power over time with storage and sign | Mean steady force alone cannot validate transient tension, drive power, temperatures or product yield |

Acceptance tolerances must be declared from instrument uncertainty, required output accuracy, temporal/spatial resolution and identified plant domain before fitting. Source plots and OEM benefit percentages do not provide those tolerances. Validation data must cover phase transitions and interruptions, not only pass averages. Public examples support method selection; no reviewed public case yet closes this project's complete aluminium operational validation packet.

## Roadmap dependency, not a deferred UI item

P1 defines phase/model-validity/recipe semantics and steady-domain rejection tests. P2 labels public comparisons by their actual evidence (usually steady/pass-average) and prohibits full-cycle claims. P3 acquires phase-labelled time series and coordinates before calibration. P4 includes a dedicated O0→O1→O2 dynamics decision gate alongside thermal/history work. P5 full-pass time/energy/quality optimisation waits for the necessary operational dynamics and data; limited steady scheduling retains its narrower label. P7 displays states already defined and validated in the core; P8–P10 preserve phase, recipe, model and event provenance through packaging/observability/read-only plant replay. Live control remains separate.

Immediate work after research closure can define the O0 schema, phase fixtures and eligibility contract using synthetic events in Codex cloud, alongside P1 numerical correction. It cannot declare plant recipes, enable thresholds, dynamic plant accuracy or automatic control established. The absence of private plant data blocks those claims, not external literature research or a reviewable architecture.
