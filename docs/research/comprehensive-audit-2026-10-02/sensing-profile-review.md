# Equipment, sensing, profile and flatness: evidence audit and development plan

Audit date: 2026-10-02. Code inspected at `caec37ef86a8b78e1eb88292ea6368bfb64bcbdc`. This is an original research/planning contribution, not an instruction to implement a controller or operate machinery. The companion [source records](sensing-profile-source-reviews.json) account for 39 assigned historical entries and 12 additional existing-code/current-locator sources. These are a subset of the master corpus; do not add their counts to the corpus without deduplication. Full-text review denotes substantive textual-body review, not reproduction of every equation image or independent replication. Partial review remains partial even when a complete document is publicly accessible.

The immediately useful baseline is a local advisory tool that explains pass state and records evidence-qualified measurements. Roll-stack, surface and free-strip shape predictions require separate calibrated physics. A realistic-looking mill scene cannot supply those missing models. Existing source counts substantially overstate the amount of calibrated, project-applicable evidence if OEM pages, numerical cross-checks and experiments are all treated alike.

## Source-specific findings that change the plan

The aluminium twin-stand AGC paper is particularly useful for the information architecture: its material-segment delay list and distinctions between actuator position and loaded gap are directly relevant to offline replay. Its practical evidence is an installation before/after comparison; no raw series or independent validation split is supplied. Its force, circular flattening and hot-steel friction formulas require independent dimensional/physical checks; the extracted Eq. 3 is incomplete or dimensionally suspect, so the rendered equation must be verified before reuse. An instrumented 0.44 mm product is a candidate historical context, not a mandatory project target. [R011, primary PDF](https://article.nadiapub.com/IJCA/vol10_no6/6.pdf)

A finite-difference segmented stack plus plate-eigenvalue model offers a plausible intermediate fidelity tier. The steel study explicitly distinguishes buckling mode from amplitude, reports near-degenerate first modes, and compares with reference calculations and ANSYS. Consequently its illustrated wave height must not be rendered as a physical prediction. Project acceptance should assess a near-degenerate mode subspace rather than exact eigenvector signs/order. [R048, primary paper](https://aimnet.it/la_metallurgia_italiana/2023/maggio/Kiakidis.pdf)

The EN AW-5083 paper provides a comparatively useful published experimental packet: composition, initial mechanical properties, reduction-dependent tensile tests, and 14 destructive surface-stress estimates. However, the inversion assumes a symmetric linear through-thickness stress distribution, the friction coefficient is not independently measured, and applied-tension cases are numerical. Its body and abstract say increasing friction decreases residual stress, while the conclusion reverses that trend. Treat the measured surface observations and the conflicting model interpretation separately. [R080, publisher text](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jmbm-2022-0297/html)

The reduced plate model has useful computational ideas but unsafe notation to copy verbatim: a quantity multiplying distance inside a cosine must be inverse length, despite its “half-wave length” description; plate membrane resultants and MPa-labelled tables need reconciliation. Its prose also contradicts tabulated amplitude/wavelength trends. A reproduction must derive its own dimensionally consistent formulation and compare with the original rendered equations before accepting numerical examples. [R087, author manuscript](https://hal.univ-lorraine.fr/hal-01503450v1/document)

The aluminium bridle study demonstrates why released shape is a different output from an in-line stress profile. It includes tension release, springback, shell-discretization checks and geometric versus strain-based I-units. The wave quantity called amplitude appears as `A/2` in its sinusoid, so importing it as a peak amplitude causes a factor-four error in the squared geometric flatness relation. Its numerical timing comparisons are hardware/case-specific, and its suggested product I-unit levels are not user requirements. [R083, author manuscript](https://hal.univ-lorraine.fr/hal-01501445/document)

A coupled local stress-relaxation model and a chained shell post-buckling model can serve different purposes. The 2009 source's local model does not predict mode/amplitude; the chained model does. Its weak bite-feedback conclusion belongs to the reported steel case and cannot establish universal one-way coupling. [R088, author manuscript](https://minesparis-psl.hal.science/hal-00509416/document)

The asymmetric laboratory aluminium profile experiments have unequal work-roll diameters and cross/shift settings. They are useful for a future geometry-specific benchmark, but not a symmetric four-high plant validation. [R082, publisher article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2017/6489769) Texture-informed asymmetric rolling can justify a later anisotropic model, but its fixed-within-pass anisotropy explicitly limits multipass extrapolation. [R086, primary PDF](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mecanique/item/10.1016/j.crme.2018.06.001.pdf)

## Per-pass information contract

This is proposed engineering design, supported by the distinctions above. A pass record should reference immutable versions of four separate objects:

1. **Material state:** alloy/composition, temper and prior history, geometry/profile, temperatures and thermal gradients if represented, strength/hardening state, surface roughness/oxide/oil condition, coil/material-coordinate mapping and uncertainty. Prediction and observation histories remain distinguishable.
2. **Equipment configuration:** stand topology, roll IDs and surveyed geometry, grinding crown/wear state, bearing/chock arrangement, available bending/shift/tilt actuators, reel geometry, fluid circuits, sensor locations and calibration versions. Changes are events, not silent overwrites.
3. **Settings and realised actuation:** intended gauge/speed/tension targets; commanded and actual cylinder positions, chamber pressures, bending/shift values, spray-valve states and fluid conditions. These are separate from material state and measured output.
4. **Bounds and acceptance:** approved equipment capacity, sensor operating envelope, model validity limits and product tolerance. Each has its own source, version and responsible owner. Never replace unknown bounds with catalogue ratings or synthetic scenario ranges.

Every measurement needs raw value/unit, transformed SI value, physical quantity identity, acquisition timestamp/timebase, quality flag, filter/bandwidth, location, calibration ID, uncertainty and material coordinate. Direction reversal exchanges entry/exit roles but does not change the physical sensor identity. Zone profiles need strip-edge tracking and zone coverage; scanning gauges additionally need scan-time/position records.

For data alignment use transported material coordinate, integrating measured speed along each span while accounting for sensor filtering and processing delay. Constant distance/speed is only a steady approximation; it fails during acceleration, stopping, reversal and changing strip storage. Compare like parcels, and flag intervals lacking adequate alignment. Sensor lag and process thermal lag must not be fitted as one unexplained delay.

## Fidelity choices and dependencies

These are qualitative cost classes, not measured project runtimes.

| Tier | Selected approach / alternative | Inputs and cost | Acceptance and restriction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0: existing synthetic explanation | Registry, SI observation helpers, scalar assumed compliance | Very low; supplied values | Provenance/units/guards. No physical accuracy claim. |
| 1: calibrated scalar gauge advisory | Nonlinear load–extension curve with explicit roll diameter/width, temperature and bearing corrections; alternative local linear tangent | Low per pass; qualified unloaded/loaded references | Held-out gauge/force comparisons with uncertainty; do not double count flattening already included in calibration. |
| 2: widthwise profile | Segmented beam/influence-function stack coupled to pressure distribution; alternative full elastic FE | Moderate; barrel/neck/chock/support data, bending/shift forces and grinding/thermal crown | Force/moment balance, interface compatibility, grid convergence, limiting beams, FE cross-check and measured loaded profile. |
| 3: latent flatness and onset | Differential elongation plus membrane compatibility and plate eigenproblem; alternative local relaxation criterion | Moderate; cross-width strip constitutive response, span boundaries/tension | Self-equilibrating residual component plus applied tension; demonstrate stable critical-load/mode subspaces. No amplitude from eigenvectors. |
| 4: released shape and residual stress | Geometrically nonlinear shell/post-buckling and explicit tension-release/leveling path; alternative harmonic weakly nonlinear model in justified domain | High; material anisotropy/cyclic law, through-thickness state and measured initial imperfections | Released/cut sample comparisons, mesh/load-path sensitivity and energy checks; no universal decoupling assumption. |
| 5: transient thermal/surface/dynamics | Resolved roll thermal field and actuator response, mixed lubrication/roughness and chatter dynamics as separate validated modules | High and data intensive; fluid/nozzle/roughness spectra/thermal maps/dynamic tests | Multi-output validation with withheld coils/regimes. Defer until relevant product requirements and data justify added degrees of freedom. |

A surrogate should approximate an independently validated tier over a declared domain, propagate its uncertainty and reject extrapolation. It cannot manufacture missing roll-stack geometry, plant bounds or emissivity calibration. Hybrid residual correction is plausible after immutable train/validation/test partitions by coil, campaign and time; random row splits of autocorrelated pass data are insufficient. A residual model needs baseline-versus-hybrid comparisons and tests on changed alloy/temper, width, roll campaign and sensor drift.

## Measurement and quality strategy

A useful minimum measurement packet for force/gauge/slip calibration contains local entry/exit thickness, width and speed, roll speed, stand forces with load-path notes, entry/exit tension, temperatures with optical context, actual settings, alloy/temper/history, roll IDs and timing information. Uncertainty must include shared errors: the same thickness gauge can affect both mass-flow inference and tension-stress conversion. Optimising to correlated measurement noise can make a weak model look accurate.

The supplier documentation supports several important exclusions. A current Achenbach page renders numerical placeholders as zero; these are not equipment specifications. [R094](https://www.achenbach.de/en/machines-equipment/optimill-rolling-mills/optimill-strip-rolling-mills/) The dedicated UniHeat description identifies induction heat generated in the work roll, despite shorthand elsewhere about strip-edge heating. [R098](https://www.achenbach.de/en/machines-equipment/technology-components/uniheat-curved-hot-edge-system/) The ABB PFTL211 example is a hot-mill looper sensor, so it is not a selected cold-strip deflector instrument. [PFTL211](https://www.abb.com/global/en/areas/automation/measurement-analytics/force-measurement/strip-tension/pressductor-pillowblock-load-cells-pftl211)

Flatness sensor outputs require distinct quantity tags: distributed zone force, derived stress, I-units, and error relative to a target are different outputs. [ABB measurement variants](https://www.abb.com/global/en/areas/automation/measurement-analytics/force-measurement/flatness-measurement/flatness-systems/flatness-measurement-system) Widthwise thickness crown/wedge, tension-distribution flatness, released wave shape, coil set, and through-thickness residual stress require separate validation observables. A product may pass one and fail another.

Temperature calibration must include alloy, wavelength, surface condition, background and geometric view. A polished, oil-cleaned sample heated to 600–800 K is informative about radiometry but outside cold oily-strip conditions. [R091](https://engineering.purdue.edu/mudawar/files/articles-all/2005/2005_3.pdf) Thermal camera colour maps and roll-temperature overlays should carry separate location/quantity tags. Do not render predicted internal energy as a measured strip surface temperature.

Surface quality also cannot be reduced to one friction coefficient. Require appropriately sampled roughness/texture, lubricant residue and oxide state, defect images/labels, inspection location and product acceptance definition. Chatter monitoring needs frequency response, mounting and labelled defect correspondence; the steel report's bands are not aluminium alarm thresholds. Its 2026 English publication has a 2025 Japanese version and explicitly identifies a revised/reprinted 2015 contribution, so these versions must not be counted as independent validation experiments. [R047](https://www.jfe-steel.co.jp/en/research/report/035/pdf/035-07.pdf)

## Benchmark and data acquisition priorities

No turnkey public dataset in this assigned subset supplies a fully aligned aluminium multipass record with alloy history, force, torque, thermal boundaries, profile, flatness and final-product labels. That is a scoped finding, not a claim that none exists anywhere.

| Priority | Evidence asset | Work possible now | Required before calibration claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | R080 published tables | Register independent measured inputs/derived outputs, conversion assumptions and conflict flags | Verify original tables/strain conventions, reconstruct uncertainty in destructive inversion; do not fit friction to the same stress points used for validation. |
| P0 | R011 architecture and before/after figures | Design replay/alignment and gauge-response benchmark contract | Obtain licensed raw time series, calibration and coil/test definitions; a plotted aggregate is not reproducible raw data. |
| P1 | R048 numerical verification cases | Specify independent beam/plate cases and mode-subspace checks | Recover missing equation glyphs and complete boundary conditions; separate numerical verification from aluminium calibration. |
| P1 | R082 lab asymmetric profile cases | Capture exact stand topology, diameters and condition matrix | Obtain alloy/temper and raw measured profiles; any figure digitisation needs uncertainty and independent checks. |
| P1 | R083/R087/R088 released-shape cases | Reproduce clearly specified numerical tests after notation audit | Tension-release geometry and measured shape data under matching conditions; no use of steel constants as aluminium cards. |
| P1 | OEM measurement documents | Build a sensor-to-quantity/correction checklist | Instrument selection, installed calibration, surveyed position, response tests and access to approved recorded data. |

User/expert inputs needed are the first alloy/temper/product family, chosen mill topology, whether profile/free flatness/surface quality are initial acceptance outputs, available recorded data and rights, and the eventual advisory host constraints. These choices determine the smallest defensible model. Progress on the registry, uncertainty schema, literature verification and synthetic replay is possible while they are unresolved.

## Milestones through advisory deployment

1. **Evidence and semantics gate:** finish deduplicated IDs, source conflicts and versioned quantity definitions. Deliver sensor/data schema plus independent SI/sign fixtures. Stop any benchmark whose quantity meaning, local geometry, or source version remains ambiguous.
2. **Offline replay gate:** ingest approved historical files into immutable raw/calibrated layers, validate timestamps and material-coordinate alignment, and report missing/invalid channels. Accept only when manually traceable example parcels agree with independently checked transformation calculations and timing uncertainty is below the pre-agreed analysis tolerance. Unknown tolerances remain explicit requirements, not invented numbers.
3. **Calibrated gauge/force gate:** select a narrow alloy/temper and equipment envelope, estimate identifiable parameters with sensitivity/rank checks, reserve independent coils, and quantify predictive intervals. Accept against product-specific error criteria agreed before fitting. Stop when good force fit hides poor slip/gauge fit, when parameter solutions are nonunique, or when model residuals show unmodelled regimes.
4. **Profile/flatness research gate:** introduce the required fidelity tier only with geometry and measured output. Deliver independent numerical verification and released-versus-loaded definitions. Gate every added observable separately; scalar force validation never qualifies shape, damage or surface predictions.
5. **Scenario/UI/3D gate:** show pass-by-pass state, settings, bounds, evidence IDs, validity and uncertainty. Use original illustrative geometry. Measurement and prediction have visibly different labels; unavailable quantities remain unavailable. Invalid/OOD scenarios fail clearly, and audit exports reproduce the exact result from model/data/config versions.
6. **Local/edge/on-prem advisory release gate:** package a reproducible offline installation with pinned dependencies, local data policy, role/access design, backup/restore, migration and rollback, resource limits and observability. Verify clean-install/replay reproducibility, corrupted-file rejection, permission handling and absence of unintended control-write paths. A disconnected workstation is the recommended initial target; edge/on-prem read-only deployment follows explicit operational requirements.
7. **Plant advisory gate:** integrate approved read-only historian/recorded streams after data-owner and OT review. Run shadow comparisons first, audit stale or missing data, version every recommendation and require a human decision. Performance monitoring should separate sensor drift, material/process shifts and model-version changes. Live feedback control is outside this roadmap's authorization and requires its own controls/safety engineering, hazard analysis, hardware validation and approval programme.

The existing sources support staged information and physics development. They do not establish a safe production schedule, calibrated industrial limits, or permission for an agent to control equipment.
