# Gap study: roll bite mechanics, friction, lubrication and surface generation in thin-gauge aluminium cold rolling

Scope: foil 6-60 um and sheet gauge down from about 1 mm; compared with `physicroll` as found on 2026-11-18 (repository read only, nothing changed there).

How to read the tags

- Read status of a source: **FT** full text read (the part read is stated), **EX** excerpt, **AB** abstract only, **SN** search snippet or bibliographic record only, **VP** vendor or trade page. Section 6 gives the status of every source; a tag is never upgraded in the text.
- **[derived]** = computed or inferred in this study from the cited source; **[run]** = obtained by running the project's own solver read-only (`PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1`, no file written in the repository); **[M]** = from memory, not verified.
- Effort: **S** up to about two days, **M** one to two weeks, **L** more than two weeks (standard-library Python, including tests and documentation).
- Band types: **LW** lengthwise bands at fixed width positions, **SL** short-lived bands, **TR** transverse marks.

## 2. State of the art

### 2.1 Foil and thin-strip bite mechanics

- **Why classical theory fails.** With circular (Hitchcock) flattening and sliding friction everywhere, the theory predicts a limiting gauge; industry rolls far below it. Johnson & Bentall showed that yield starts at two separated points, so reduction happens in two zones with a no-slip zone between them [S1 FT p.120; S2 FT p.509]. Fleck & Johnson put the failure of conventional models at "thinner than about 100 um" [S2 FT p.508]. Shigaki et al. call results suspect once the deformed radius reaches twice the initial one, and divergent beyond [S15 EX, introduction].
- **Fleck & Johnson (1987).** Homogeneous elastic-perfectly-plastic strip; roll as an elastic foundation, but only for the *perturbation* of a Hertzian (elliptical) pressure: `p(x) = p0*sqrt(1-(x/a0)^2) - K_p*Delta_b`, with `K_p = 8E_R*/(3*pi*a0)` and `K_q = (2/3)K_p`; they state that a plain mattress gives a parabolic pressure and that "this discrepancy was found to be significant" [S2 FT p.510, eqs 3, 3a]. Seven zones A-G: elastic slip at entry, plastic reduction, contained plastic no-slip, elastic unloading no-slip, elastic slip, plastic reduction at exit, elastic exit. The slip equation is `s/V_R = eps_strip - eps_roll + xi` (creep ratio `xi`) [S2 FT p.511, eq 8]. Groups: `W_bar = W*E_R*/(R*Y_s^2)`, `H_bar = 2*b0*E_R*/(mu*R*Y_s)`, `U = mu*E_R*/Y_s`; at the onset of reduction (r = 0), as `H_bar -> 0`, `W_bar -> 81*pi/H_bar^2` [S2 FT p.513-516].
- **Fleck, Johnson, Mear & Zhang (1992).** The mattress is "abandoned"; rolls are elastic half-spaces [S1 FT p.120]. Equations: strip equilibrium `b*dsigma_x/dx + (sigma_x + p)*db/dx + q = 0` (1); yield `sigma_x + p = Y_s` (3a); roll shape `b(x) = b0 - (a0^2-x^2)/(2R) + (2/(pi*E_R*))*Int p(x')*ln|(a0+x')/(x-x')| dx'` (8); slip zones `q = +-mu*p` (9a); no-slip `eps_R - eps_s = xi` (10) with roll surface strain `eps_R = -((1-2nu_R)/(1-nu_R))*p/E_R* - (2/(pi*E_R*))*Int q/(x-x') dx'` (11); elastic no-slip approximately `sigma_x + 0.62*p = C` for aluminium on steel (14a); plastic reduction `-b*dp/dx + Y_s*db/dx + alpha*mu*p = 0` (15); contained plastic no-slip `b1*dp/dx = q` (16). Solved with about 100 triangular pressure elements and closed-form influence coefficients (30)-(32) [S1 FT p.121-123, 129]. Three regimes: I conventional (Hitchcock adequate at 140 um in their example), II neutral zone of contained plastic flow, III with elastic unloading and a sharp pressure peak just after the neutral zone; pressure peaks up to about 16 `Y_s` at 13 um entry gauge (R 89 mm, r 50 %, mu 0.03, `Y_s` 230 MPa) [S1 FT Fig. 1, p.120-121]. No limiting gauge. Results collapse on a master curve `W_bar*U^-0.70*r^-0.70` against `B0*U^-1.24*r^-0.5` (`B0 = b*E_R*^2/(R*Y_s^2)`); the regime I/II boundary lies at `B0*U^-1.24*r^-0.5 ~ 6`, i.e. critical inlet gauge `h_0c/R ~ 12*r^0.5*mu^1.24*(Y_s/E_R*)^0.76` (29) [S1 FT p.126-127, Fig. 6]. The earlier mattress model "underpredicts rolling load by up to a factor of 2" and, at r = 10 %, torque by an order of magnitude; for one fully specified case (R 89 mm, b0 0.01 mm, r 25 %, `Y_s` 230 MPa, `E_R*` 230 GPa, `E_s*` 80 GPa, mu 0.03, no tensions) the half-space solution gives `W_bar` = 118, a perturbation solution 120, the mattress 62 [S1 FT p.128, Fig. 9]. The elastic zones at entry and exit are "very short and can be neglected" for r > 10 % [S1 FT p.123]. Strain hardening in the final passes of a foil mill is small, "typically 4 per cent through the penultimate stand" [S1 FT p.128]. Stated weakest point: constant Coulomb friction, although reduction is known to depend on speed; film about 0.1 um, comparable with roughness [S1 FT p.128].
- **Le & Sutcliffe (2001), "robust model".** Same physics, but the pressure in the neutral zone follows explicitly from the sticking condition instead of Fleck's inverse method, and circular-arc and heavily deformed cases are treated by one algorithm; results agree with Fleck et al.; equal entry and exit tensions act like a reduction of the yield stress by the tension [S4 AB]. This is the form to implement.
- **Queensland group.** Langlands & McElwain (2002): modified-Hertzian approximation by perturbation methods [S17 SN]. Langlands, McElwain & Domanti (2004): approximate solution of the influence-function model using an inverse Hilbert transform for the pressure in the flat region, valid with or without a flat region [S18 AB]; a search summary states that it includes the elastic end regions Fleck neglected [SN]. Domanti, Edwards, Thomas & Chefneux (1994) applied foil models to thin steel strip and temper rolling [S20 SN]. The MISG report shows that the pure mattress form `(h - G2)p' + mu*p = x` cannot show the boundary-layer behaviour of the industrial influence-function model and was dropped for that reason [S21 EX].
- **Dixon & Yuen (BHP).** Combined analytical and numerical solution of the slab equations with circular or computed non-circular roll profile, built for on-line force prediction [S19 AB]; the 1995 conference paper was not found.
- **CEMEF, ArcelorMittal, Liege line.** Influence-function (Jortner, Meindl) or FE roll deformation coupled to a slab strip; the roll profile can become locally concave near the neutral point ("numerical punching" or "trough"), which is handled by allowing the profile at most to become flat (Fleck & Johnson), by Matsumoto's small elastic thickening, and by under-relaxation of the profile (weights 0.5 down to 0.1 or less at high loads) [S14 FT sec. 4.10.3.4]. Shigaki, Nakhoul & Montmitonnet compare a regularised stick/slip friction law with a directly imposed no-slip contained plastic zone and show that the choice changes the results [S15 EX]. Dbouk et al.: a slab model with roll circumferential displacements and dynamic relaxation that converges in under a second from heavy tandem reductions to temper passes below 0.5 %, checked against FE [S16 AB]. Krimpelstaetter's skin-pass model (elastic zones inside the bite, tangential roll displacement) is named by Boemer as probably the best 1D model [S14 FT sec. 2.3.1]. 2D FE (Lam3, Abaqus, Metafor ALE) serves as reference; for 2D roll deformation FE is "practically abandoned" because of cost [S15 EX].
- **Experiments on the bite.** Plasticine strip between elastomer rolls confirms a short reduction zone at the inlet and a nearly flat central zone, but not the predicted exit reduction; for the thinnest strips the measured load is almost an order of magnitude below the theory [S22 AB, aggregator copy with dropped words]. Keife et al. reinterpret foil experiments in which the strip profile looks concave (thickening) and attribute it to what happens after the rolls stop [S23 AB].
- **Elastic entry and exit zones, recovery.** Negligible for load at r > 10 % [S1 FT]; they dominate in temper passes (models of Krimpelstaetter, Dbouk et al.) [S14 FT, S16 AB]. At foil gauge the elastic thickness recovery is of order `Y_s/E_s*` of the gauge, about 0.3 % (0.05-0.1 um at 18-30 um) [derived]; what matters is the *longitudinal* elastic strain of strip and roll surface in the no-slip condition (eqs 10-12).

### 2.2 Closed-gap rolling and roll kiss

- Vendor and trade texts: once the work-roll faces touch outside the strip, most of the extra force passes roll to roll; work-roll bending is used to hold the faces apart [S42 VP; project sources S8, S16, S17]. No peer-reviewed quantification for an aluminium foil mill was found (section 7).
- Model class in the literature (thin steel strip): influence-function roll stack with work-roll bending, work-roll/backup and work-roll/strip flattening, plus work-roll/work-roll flattening over an unknown kiss length; contact set found by iteration (negative contact forces released). Ogawa et al. (1991, 12-high cluster mill): in roll-kiss rolling (0.05 -> 0.03 mm in their example) work-roll bending has almost no effect on flatness, while backup crown and intermediate-roll bending and shift do [S26 EX]. Jiang & Tieu (4-high, slit-beam influence functions, Foppl flattening for the kiss, a compatibility condition between the deformed work-roll profile and the centre-line flattening and gauge, their eq. 20, whose operators were lost in the text extraction): for 0.2 -> 0.12 mm steel, 160 mm wide, mu 0.1, the kiss force is 65.3 kN against 596 kN rolling force (11 %), and the strip crown falls from 45.6 to 36.5 um; on the Hille 100 laboratory mill the kiss share averaged 15.0 % and 15.6 % at 0.27 and 0.15 m/s; higher friction and lower speed raise the kiss force; strip width changes the kiss length and the edge wear [S24 FT sections 2.6, 4.1, the section with Figs 18-19, and 5; S25 EX].
- A plant paper on foil-mill flatness control (Sumitomo Light Metal) builds actuator models from plant experiments [S41 AB]; contents not read.

### 2.3 Doubled (pack) rolling

- Two sheets rolled together double the effective thickness, which reduces roll flattening and allows thinner foil; outer faces are bright (roll contact), inner faces matt [S28 EX].
- Matt surface: roughening by grain-scale inhomogeneous deformation and shear banding; modelled by plane-strain FE with a strength distribution over grains (isotropic plasticity, strengths from EBSD orientation data); roughness scales with grain size and depends on the number of grains through the thickness; 3D effects are significant; agreement with measured matt roughness at 50 % reduction is "encouraging" [S28 EX]. A crystal-plasticity variant links the matt corrugations to texture and to fracture initiation [S29 SN]. Thinner foil needs a smoother matt side to avoid strength loss and pinholes [S28 EX].
- No published bite model of a doubled pass (two strips, oil between them) was found; the references above treat the pack as one body under frictionless flat platens.

### 2.4 Friction and lubrication

- **Three generations of models** [S14 FT sec. 2.3]: (1) Coulomb or Tresca friction in the whole bite; (2) mixed lubrication: Wilson & Walowit inlet film; Tsao & Sargent; Sutcliffe & Johnson and Sheu & Wilson with asperity flattening under bulk strain and the (averaged, Patir-Cheng) Reynolds equation, "high-speed" models in which the oil pressure equals the interface pressure in the work zone; Wilson & Chang "low-speed" models; combined by Lin, Marsault and Wilson (Marsault's Lam2DTribo, the basis of Metalub); Qiu et al. finite-difference solution; (3) film formation and starvation, micro-plasto-hydrodynamic lubrication, thermal coupling (Stephany, Carretta, Boemer: Metalub with thermo-elastoviscoplastic strip, piezoviscous oil, non-circular roll, starvation). Even the best model still needs manual adjustment of film thickness and boundary friction when starvation or MPH lubrication occurs [S14 FT abstract].
- **Asperity flattening under bulk strain.** With the substrate at yield, asperities are crushed far beyond the one-third contact area of a static indentation; the flattening rate depends on contact-area ratio and on the plateau-to-valley pressure difference over the yield stress, and falls with asperity slope; evolution depends on bulk strain, not strain rate (Sutcliffe 1988; Wilson & Sheu 1988) [S9 EX sec. 4.2]. Long wavelengths flatten faster than short ones; in foil the small thickness-to-wavelength ratio makes crushing easier [S8 EX; S3 EX].
- **Friction closure.** `mu = A*mu_c + (1-A)*mu_v` (or the friction-factor form); the contact term dominates; `mu_c` is "to a certain extent a fitting parameter", typically 0.1 (or m = 0.2) [S9 EX eq. 4.13-4.14]. Valley shear from the Eyring model `tau_v = tau_0*asinh(eta*u_slide/(tau_0*h_v))` [S7 EX eq. 4-6]; the kerosene foil oil is Eyring-like at rolling pressures and shear rates [S12 AB].
- **Measurements on aluminium.** Experimental mill: friction depends mainly on `Lambda_s` and on reduction; which surface is rough does not matter; oil temperature 40-60 C is secondary; about 0.09 at the lowest speeds, below 0.01 at the highest; transition where the film is of the order of the additive molecule length, 3 nm [S6 AB]. Conditions as restated by Le & Sutcliffe: rolls R = 30 mm, Vitrea 68 (`eta0` 0.077 Pa s, `alpha` 2.2e-8 /Pa), rough strip 0.5 um on smooth rolls 0.06 um, entry 0.35 mm, 25 %, film 5 to 500 nm (the manuscript prints "um") [S7 EX]. A single-wavelength model with constant boundary friction 0.09 cannot reproduce this; a two-wavelength roughness model with boundary friction that falls with film thickness and rises with reduction (measured on a strip-drawing rig: AA1050 work hardened, `Y_ps` 161 MPa, 0.016 m/s, films 1-100 nm, 12.5 % and 25 %) does [S7 EX]. The later multi-scale model ties contact friction to the theoretical film and to secondary-scale roll roughness and agrees with laboratory and industrial mill trials [S32 AB via search summary].
- **Foil, coupled models.** Sutcliffe & Montmitonnet: asperity and oil-pressure evolution through the bite, slab strip, FE roll, friction on asperity tops by Coulomb (`mu_a` = 0.1, capped at the shear yield stress) or Tresca (`m_a` = 0.25), viscous valleys, friction made proportional to roll slope in the sticking zone. Case: 50 -> 25 um, yield 150 MPa, tensions 20/20 MPa, R 135 mm, 5-40 m/s, combined roughness 0.3 um, wavelength 30 um, oil 2.5e-3 Pa s and 1.2e-8 /Pa; `Lambda_s` 0.22 to 1.76. As speed falls the contact area and friction rise, a flat zone appears below about 20 m/s (Coulomb) or 30 m/s (Tresca), the load rises steeply (French summary: up to a factor 40 between 40 and 10 m/s with Coulomb friction) and forward slip rises from about 5 % to 45 %; Coulomb "snowballs", Tresca does not. The inlet is very short compared with the bite, so the tribology can be solved in the inlet only, which is simpler and more robust [S3 EX]. Le & Sutcliffe (2002): robust bite plus mixed-lubrication model against industrial mill trials: load in reasonable agreement for thin strip and foil; forward slip over-estimated for foil, "greatly improved if a variation of friction through the bite is considered" [S5 AB]. Keife & Jonsater: a Stribeck-type curve for foil rolling from a model with roll-asperity indentation [S13 AB].
- **Film thickness.** `h_s = 6*eta0*alpha*U/(theta0*(1 - exp(-alpha*Y)))` (Wilson & Walowit as restated) [S9 EX eq. 4.1; S8 EX]. First asperity contact below `Lambda_s` about 3; substantial contact and a good roll imprint need `Lambda_s` below about 1; industrial rolling is commonly at `Lambda_s` 0.01-0.5 [S9 EX; S3 EX]. For large `Lambda_s` the surface roughens [S9 EX]. Measured films are up to a factor two below smooth theory where film exceeds roughness (uneven oil supply), in reasonable agreement with the mixed theory otherwise [S11 AB]. Inlet slope: the undeformed-roll slope is used, the deformed-roll slope being "not very different" in the foil case [S3 EX footnote]. In the S3 case the film is about 66 nm at 5 m/s and 530 nm at 40 m/s [derived from `Lambda_s`*0.3 um].
- **Speed effect.** At fixed load, more speed gives more reduction (industrial practice) [S2 FT p.511; S1 FT p.128]. Neat-oil mills show falling load with rising speed through film growth; the opposite occurs where temperature rises so far that the oil degrades [S36 FT introduction].
- **Starvation and film formation.** Treated in third-generation models (inlet meniscus, plate-out, dynamic concentration); mostly for emulsions on steel [S14 FT sec. 2.2.4.1; S36 FT]. Nothing specific to neat-oil foil mills was found.
- **Micro-plasto-hydrodynamic lubrication (MPHL).** Oil trapped in surface pockets is drawn into the contact areas at higher sliding speed and viscosity (Mizuno & Okamoto; Azushima; Bech); pit evolution models by Sutcliffe, Le & Ahmed and Lo & Wilson; in Metalub it still needs FE asperity simulations [S14 FT sec. 2.2.4.3; S9 EX; publication list SN]. Whether "contact" areas are dry, boundary or thin-film lubricated is described as an open question [S9 EX].
- **Boundary films, additives, transfer layers.** Plane-strain compression of aluminium with stearic acid or aluminium stearate (up to 0.5 %): on the first indent hydrodynamic effects dominate and the additive does not change friction; after several indents a transfer layer builds on the tool, containing aluminium stearate formed on fresh metal; it lowers friction more at higher speed and concentration [S31 AB via search summary]. Surface oxide cracks transverse to rolling near the bite entry and metal extrudes through the cracks; below a critical reduction of about 13 % the extruded metal does not reach the roll [S30 SN]. Thermal limits of additive films are in the earlier project report (`process-temperatures.md`, items T15, T27: desorption with temperature; ball-on-disc failure of a kerosene/fatty-acid blend at 150-155 C).
- **Forward slip as a friction measure.** Roll force, torque and forward slip measured on commercially pure aluminium; friction rises with reduction and falls with speed; heavier oil gives negative forward slip (onset of full film); five published forward-slip friction formulas are called unreliable [S37 SN]. Laboratory data with numbers: section 5, row V9.

### 2.5 Surface generation

- **Imprint and transfer.** After the first pass on freshly ground rolls the strip surface nearly conforms to the roll surface; later passes change it little (AA1200, 0.4 mm, two passes of about 50 %, industrial mill) [S8 EX]. Ground rolls have a longitudinal lay that is transferred; pits on foil appear where an oil film separates roll and foil [S9 EX]. Roll and strip share self-affine roughness statistics from 100 um down to 50 nm [S38 SN]. A micro-mechanical roughness-transfer model for cold and temper rolling is a current research project [S46 SN]. Localised roll grinding deviations transfer to strip thickness and flatness (3D roll-stack FE) [S43 SN].
- **Bright and dull.** Mixed regime prints the bright roll; full film gives "hydrodynamic pits" and a dull surface; in foil the surface quality is fully determined by lubrication [S33 FT introduction]. Laboratory rolling of unalloyed aluminium: more hydropitting with higher viscosity, higher speed and rougher rolls; at 59 % reduction no hydropitting but horseshoe-shaped forward-slip marks, more with smooth rolls and with the heavier oil [S34 FT].
- **Herringbone.** Lubricant test to herringbone onset with continuous force and torque records [S35 AB]; patent description: after very heavy reductions (about 55 % or more) with ground rolls, attributed to stick-slip between thick-film and boundary contact [S39 SN; already in the project].
- **Smudge, fines, pick-up.** Debris generation rises with reduction, abrasive at light and adhesive at heavy reductions, off the Archard law at high reduction (Reich, Epp & Gantzer); debris scales with the distance the roll smears over the strip, i.e. with the slip either side of the neutral point (patent) [S39 SN]. Roll coating state changes surface and debris [SN].
- **Pinholes.** Likelihood rises as gauge falls; causes classified by microscopy and laboratory rolling, with emphasis on cast-stock defects [S40 AB]; on the matt side of doubled foil, roughness comparable with the gauge [S28 EX]. No bite-mechanics model of pinhole formation was found.

### 2.6 Where the literature contradicts, qualifies or supports the project's documents

1. **"No benchmark with fully known inputs was found"** (`band-diagnosis.md` 10.2, 14; `foil_bite.py` docstring). Fleck et al. Fig. 9 is one (all inputs stated; `W_bar` = 118 half-space, 120 perturbation, 62 mattress) and the Fig. 1/Fig. 4 series (U = 30) is another [S1 FT]. On Fig. 9 the project's `solve_mattress_bite` returns `W_bar` = 49.8 (1.02 kN/mm against 2.42 kN/mm), i.e. 0.42 of the reference and below Fleck & Johnson's own mattress value [run; dimensional values derived]. On the regime I case of Fig. 1 (140 um, 50 %) it returns `W_bar` = 46 where the r = 50 % curve of Fig. 4 reads about 50 by eye [run; figure read by eye].
2. **Lineage of the mattress model.** Fleck & Johnson used `K_p = 8E*/(3*pi*a0)` = 0.849 `E*/a0`, applied to the plastic-reduction perturbation of a Hertzian pressure, and rejected the plain mattress; the project uses a plain mattress with `(3*pi/8)E*/a` = 1.178 `E*/a` (the reciprocal number). Fleck et al. then abandoned the mattress because it under-predicts load by up to a factor 2 and torque by up to a factor 10 [S2 FT p.510; S1 FT p.128]. The project's "order of magnitude only" label is fair; "in the spirit of Fleck & Johnson" overstates the kinship.
3. **Forward-slip flag.** `FORWARD_SLIP_IMPLAUSIBLY_LARGE_NO_ELASTIC_STRIP_ZONES` blames missing elastic strip zones. Fleck et al. found those zones negligible for r > 10 % [S1 FT]. Large forward slip is what a Coulomb model with a no-slip zone gives (the entry share of the reduction shrinks as gauge falls [S1 FT p.124]); Sutcliffe & Montmitonnet compute 5 % to 45 % and describe such values as met in practice [S3 EX]; mill trials show that the constant-friction model over-estimates slip and that friction varying through the bite repairs it [S5 AB]. The remedy in the literature is a friction distribution (higher towards the exit), not elastic zones.
4. **Regime labels.** The project calls `Lambda` < 1 BOUNDARY "[V: Sutcliffe 2002]". Sutcliffe's text makes `Lambda_s` below about 1 the condition for a good imprint within the *mixed* regime, and Sutcliffe & Montmitonnet give 0.01-0.5 as the common industrial window [S9 EX; S3 EX]. Measured friction on aluminium falls from about 0.09 to below 0.01 inside `Lambda_s` of roughly 0.01 to 1 [S6 AB; S7 EX]. So "every schedule pass is in the boundary regime (film ratio 0.0139 to 0.180)" describes normal operation, and the heat-streak rule "boundary regime" fires on it. The placeholder `exp(-Lambda/1.0)` keeps friction near 0.09 over most of that window; neither its level nor its slope is supported.
5. **Inlet angle at foil gauge.** `theta0 = sqrt(draft/(R*contact_radius_ratio))` with the mattress ratio `L^2/(R*draft)` is the mean slope of the whole bite. The literature uses the undeformed-roll inlet slope [S3 EX]. With ratios 2.1, 3.3 and 11.1 (demo passes F1, F2, ALT2) the project's film is thicker by 1.45, 1.8 and 3.3 times [derived].
6. **"The published high-friction foil case, 50 -> 25 um at friction 0.10"** (10.2). In that paper 0.1 is the friction on asperity contact areas, weighted by the contact-area ratio and capped at the shear yield stress; it is not a nominal Coulomb coefficient of 0.10 over the bite [S3 EX]. The rejection says nothing about that case.
7. **Rejection when `mu*p` exceeds shear yield.** The literature caps the shear stress at the shear yield (Orowan; Sutcliffe & Montmitonnet) or uses a friction factor [S14 FT sec. 2.3.1; S3 EX]. A gap, not an error.
8. **Supported.** (a) Eq. 29 of Fleck et al. puts the onset of a neutral zone for the demo mill (R 135 mm, 175 MPa, mu 0.03, r 0.4) at 54-63 um entry gauge, and at about 118 um for mu 0.05 [derived]; this agrees with Hitchcock rejecting the 50 -> 30 um pass and with the foil regime of the mattress model there. (b) Constant flow stress in the final foil passes is reasonable (4 % hardening) [S1 FT]. (c) The film formula with `Y` alone is what Sutcliffe prints [S9 EX]. (d) Force steeply rising with friction: the master curve gives a load exponent in friction of roughly 2 to 2.6 at fixed gauge and reduction in regimes II-III [derived, slope read by eye from S1 Fig. 6a]; the project's probe (0.959 -> 2.49 kN/mm for mu 0.03 -> 0.05) is an exponent of 1.9 [derived].
9. **Closed gap.** The project's onset figures rest on trade sources; no peer-reviewed aluminium-foil figure was found to confirm or correct them.

## 7. Looked for and not found

- A peer-reviewed model or measurement of roll kiss in an **aluminium foil mill** (onset gauge, share of load through the kiss, effect on flatness). Only thin steel strip on laboratory mills and vendor descriptions.
- Measured **forward slip, load and torque tables for foil passes below 50 um** with all inputs. The one mill point with numbers is Fleck et al. Fig. 8 (roll radius not stated); the foil mill trials of Le & Sutcliffe (2002) are behind a paywall.
- A measured **friction coefficient for foil passes** other than 0.026 (inferred, 73 -> 31 um, AA1100) and "about 0.03" (Davy-McKee, quoted by Fleck et al.).
- Measured **oil film thickness in foil rolling** with numbers; a mill curve of **gauge against speed** at constant load.
- A closed-form **contact-area or friction law `A(Lambda)`** ready to code; the fitted functions of Wilson & Sheu and Sutcliffe (1988) were not read.
- Full texts of Le & Sutcliffe (2001, 2002), Langlands & McElwain (2002), Langlands et al. (2004), Dixon & Yuen (1995), Domanti et al. (1994), Zhang (1995), Marsault's thesis, Sutcliffe & Johnson (1990), sections 4.3.2-4.6 of Sutcliffe's chapter.
- A **bite model for doubled rolling** (two sheets with oil between them); only matt-surface models.
- **Starvation** in neat-oil aluminium foil mills; a model linking nozzle oil flow to inlet film.
- A quantitative model of **herringbone**, of **roll coating or pick-up** growth, of **smudge** generation, or of **pinhole** formation linked to bite mechanics.
- Roughness **transfer ratios** for aluminium foil as a function of film ratio and reduction (only the qualitative statement that the strip conforms after one pass).
- Access limits: the page-fetch tool could not reach mdpi.com, orbi.uliege.be or the book mirrors (hosts outside the session's allowed domains); HAL and core.ac.uk refused direct downloads. Where a direct download worked (fleckmech.org, orbi PDF, intechopen, utwente, vtechworks) the file was read locally.
